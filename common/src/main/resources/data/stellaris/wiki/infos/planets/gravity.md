Gravity is not the same everywhere, and it is one of the first things you will notice when you step off the lander.

[item=stellaris:gravity_manipulator]

Each world pulls with its own strength, given in m/s² : 
- [ref=stellaris:planets/earth]Earth[ref] : 9.81 
- [ref=stellaris:planets/moon]The Moon[ref] : 1.62, about six times weaker 
- Earth Orbit : 0.5



**What changes** 

Gravity is not only a jump height. Three things move together when it drops : 
- You accelerate downwards more slowly, so you jump higher and hang in the air longer. 
- You can fall further before you take any damage at all. 
- What damage a fall does deal is reduced.

On the Moon this combination is generous. A drop that would break your legs at home is a short hop, and it is easy to get used to it — which is exactly the problem the first time you come back to Earth carrying a full inventory.

It is not just you, either. Arrows, thrown potions, falling blocks, dropped items, experience orbs, boats, minecarts and primed TNT all fall at the local rate. Arrows in particular travel much flatter on the Moon and drop off far later than you will expect.



**Changing it** 

A [ref=stellaris:machines/gravity_manipulator]Gravity Manipulator[ref] overrides gravity for the chunk it stands in, anywhere from 0 to 20, for as long as it has power. That is how a space station is made to feel like home, and how you keep your footing somewhere that has none.

Cut the power and the chunk falls straight back to the planet's natural value.
